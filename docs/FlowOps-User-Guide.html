Part 1Introduction

1.1 What FlowOps Is

FlowOps is Quinn's operations management platform for tracking inventory as it moves through the warehouse — from the moment a consigner's truck arrives, through unpacking, sorting, lotting, and final auction pickup. It is a web application that works on desktop computers at front desk and office stations, and on tablets/phones on the warehouse floor (it can be installed as a mobile app — a PWA).

Every physical rack of goods gets a digital record in FlowOps: a rack code, a QR label, a current stage, a location (zone), and a full history of everything that has happened to it. Every truck that arrives becomes a "delivery" record. Every customer who consigns goods becomes a "consigner" record. All of this is shared in real time — when one person updates something, everyone else sees the change immediately, with no refresh needed.

1.2 Why FlowOps Exists

Before FlowOps, tracking a rack's progress depended on paper tags, whiteboards, and people remembering where things were. That worked when the warehouse was small and quiet — it breaks down fast once volume grows, multiple auctions run in parallel, and staff change shifts. FlowOps exists to solve specific, recurring problems:

  • "Where is this consigner's stuff?" — Front desk and management can look up any consigner and instantly see every delivery, every rack, and its current stage and location.
  • "Why hasn't this been lotted yet?" — Every rack's time-in-stage is tracked automatically. Racks that sit too long are automatically flagged on the Needs Attention page.
  • "Are we about to miss an auction deadline?" — Auction dates are attached to deliveries and auction colors. The Dashboard warns when racks won't be Ready in time.
  • "Who moved this and when?" — Every status change is logged with a timestamp and the user who made it, visible on the rack's History panel.
  • "Is the lotting room overloaded?" — Stage Pressure and Stage Velocity panels show which stage of the pipeline is becoming a bottleneck before it causes a backlog.

1.4 Core Concepts

These terms appear throughout FlowOps and this guide. Understanding them is the foundation for everything else.

The Pipeline (6 Stages)

Every rack moves through the same sequence of stages, left to right. A rack cannot skip a stage on its own — staff advance it manually using the "Move to {Next Stage} →" button as work is completed.

StageBadgeWhat happens hereWho works itTypical time limit
Unpacking & SortingUnpacking & SortingItems are unloaded from the truck onto a rack and sorted into lotsUnpacker / Sorter5 business days
SortedSortedRack is fully sorted and waiting to be picked up for lottingSorter → Lotter24 business hours
LottingLottingItems are being organized into auction lotsLotter16 business hours (~2 days)
ReadyReadyLotted and waiting for its auction date (a "waiting" stage — expected to sit)—14 business days
PickupPickupStaged in the Pick-Up zone for the auction floor (a "waiting" stage)Pickup7 business days
CompletedCompletedRack's job is done — left the building for the auction floor——

Ready and Pickup are called "waiting stages" — sitting in them is normal (the items are waiting for their scheduled auction), so they are excluded from bottleneck and overdue warnings. Every other stage has a time limit; exceeding it triggers a Needs Attention flag.

Racks

A rack is the basic unit FlowOps tracks — a physical cart/shelf of a consigner's items. Each rack has a unique rack code (e.g. RC-0042), a status (one of the six pipeline stages above), a priority, an optional zone (physical location), and a link back to the delivery and consigner it belongs to. A rack can occasionally hold items from more than one consigner — this is shown as a mixed tag.

Deliveries

A delivery represents one truck/drop-off event from a consigner. Deliveries are either:

  • Walk-in — the truck has already arrived; created on the spot by Front Desk or Unpacking staff.
  • Scheduled — booked ahead of time for a future date, then "arrives" later.

Each delivery has a delivery code (e.g. DEL-0008) and moves through its own simple status sequence: Scheduled → Arrived → Processing → Complete.

Consigners

A consigner is the customer who brought items in to be sold. Each consigner is identified by name and, usually, a J-Number (Quinn's internal customer/job number, e.g. J-10294). FlowOps automatically builds a consigner directory from delivery records, and tracks every consigner's history, active racks, and notes.

Zones

A zone is a physical location in the warehouse or gallery — e.g. W4, G1, B (Lotting Room), C (Sorting Room), H (Hallway), or PU (Pick-Up). Zones can be assigned a "purpose" — holding an active delivery, a scheduled delivery, an auction color staging area, or simply reserved. Racks are assigned to zones so anyone can find them physically.

Auction Colors

Quinn's runs auctions in color-coded cycles: Red Orange Yellow Green Blue Black. A rack or a whole zone can be tagged with one of these colors to mark which auction run it belongs to, and each color can have a date set on the Dashboard so everyone knows the deadline for that run.

Priority, Hold & Needs Attention

ConceptMeaning
high priorityMarked urgent — typically set when a rack moves to Sorted. Shown with an amber badge, or a pulsing red badge if it's also overdue.
low priorityLower urgency than normal — no rush.
HOLDWork on this rack is intentionally paused, with a reason (e.g. "Waiting on remaining boxes," "Awaiting director review"). Held racks do not count as overdue.

Notes & Photos

Any rack, delivery, or consigner can have operational notes pinned to it — short messages visible to everyone (e.g. "Missing 2 boxes, consigner will drop off Friday"). Deliveries also support photo uploads for documenting condition on arrival or issues found during processing.

Labels, QR Codes & the Print Queue

Every rack has a printable label containing its rack code, consigner/J-number, and a QR code linking back to the rack's page. Labels can be printed individually or queued up (the Print Queue) and printed in a batch. Scanning a rack's QR code with the Scan page opens that rack instantly — no typing required.

2.2 Warehouse Staff (Unpacker / Sorter / Lotter / Pickup)

Warehouse Staff are the hands-on roles that physically move and process racks. Most warehouse employees hold one role matching the station they work, though some are granted multiple roles (e.g. a cross-trained employee may have both Sorter and Lotter).

Unpacker

  • Job: Unload trucks, build new racks, attach them to the correct consigner/delivery, and print/queue labels.
  • Home page: Create Rack (/unpack).
  • Sees in Racks queue: Unpacking & Sorting and Sorted racks only.
  • Can: Create racks, advance racks, set hold/notes, print labels, scan QR codes.

Sorter

  • Job: Sort items within a rack into lots, mark a rack "Sorted" with the correct priority.
  • Home page: Racks (/racks).
  • Sees in Racks queue: Unpacking & Sorting and Ready racks.
  • Can: Create racks, advance racks (including the Sorted priority step), hold/notes, scan.

Lotter

  • Job: Pull racks from the Sorted queue, build auction lots, mark racks Ready.
  • Home page: Lotting Queue (/lotting).
  • Sees in Racks queue: Sorted, Lotting, Ready racks.
  • Can: Create racks, advance racks, hold/notes, scan.

Pickup

  • Job: Stage racks in the Pick-Up zone for the auction floor and mark racks Completed when collected.
  • Home page: Pick-Up (redirects to the PU zone).
  • Sees in Racks queue: Ready and Pickup racks.
  • Can: Advance/Complete racks, hold/notes, scan. (Cannot create racks.)
Best PracticeAlways use the Scan page on your phone to open a rack rather than searching by code — it's faster and avoids typos.
Common MistakeAdvancing a rack to the next stage before the physical work is actually done. FlowOps timestamps the move immediately — if the rack isn't really sorted/lotted yet, the next person in line wastes time looking for work that isn't ready.

2.3 Coordinators (Front Desk)

Coordinators are the first point of contact when a consigner's truck arrives, and the people who answer "where is my stuff?" questions over the phone or at the counter.

  • Home page: Front Desk Home (/front-desk).
  • Primary screens: Front Desk Home, Deliveries, Consigners, Search.
  • Can: Register walk-in and scheduled deliveries, look up consigners, view all racks and zones, edit delivery details, add notes/photos, add zones for deliveries.
  • Cannot: Advance, revert, hold, or delete racks (view-only on rack cards and rack detail — no "Move to..." button appears). Cannot delete deliveries. Cannot access Dashboard, Reports, Dumpsters, or User Management.
TipIf a consigner calls asking about their items, use the consigner lookup search box on the Front Desk Home page — it searches by name or J-Number and shows their delivery status directly.

2.4 Supervisors

Supervisors use the Admin role for day-to-day floor oversight. They are typically the people who resolve escalations from the Needs Attention page, rebalance zones, and step in when a stage becomes a bottleneck.

  • Home page: Dashboard (/).
  • Primary screens: Dashboard, Needs Attention, Racks (all stages), Zones, Lotting Queue.
  • Can do everything a warehouse role can do, for any rack regardless of stage — including reverting a rack to a previous stage and deleting racks/deliveries.
  • Special responsibility: Reviewing the Operational Alerts on the Dashboard each morning and reassigning staff/zones to clear bottlenecks.

2.5 Managers

Managers also use the Admin role, with a focus on business-level oversight rather than moment-to-moment floor operations.

  • Home page: Dashboard (/).
  • Primary screens: Dashboard (KPIs, stage efficiency), Reports (CSV exports, backup & restore), Consigners (activity, quality flags), Dumpsters (trash billing).
  • Typical weekly tasks: Export CSV reports for billing/analysis, review consigner "Frequently held" / "Slow to process" flags, take a data backup (4.22).

2.6 Administrators

Administrators hold the Admin role and additionally manage User Management — who has accounts and what roles they're granted. This is usually IT staff or operations leadership.

  • Home page: Dashboard (/).
  • Primary screens: User Management (/admin/users), plus full access to every page in the system.
  • Can: Invite new users by email, assign/revoke any of the six roles per user, set each user's default role on login.
Best PracticeGrant the minimum roles a person needs to do their job. Someone cross-trained on two stations (e.g. Sorter + Lotter) can be granted both roles and will get a role switcher in the header — but front desk staff should never be granted Admin just to "see more."

Part 3System Navigation

3.1 Logging In & Switching Roles

Logging In

  1. Open FlowOps in a browser (or tap the installed app icon on a phone/tablet — it can be installed as a Progressive Web App for an app-like experience).
  2. If not signed in, you'll see the login screen. Enter your work email and password.
  3. New users receive an email invitation with a magic link to set their password the first time — click the link, set a password, and you're in.
  4. After login, FlowOps takes you to your role's home page automatically (see the table in Part 2.1).

Switching Roles (multi-role users only)

If your account has been granted more than one role, a role switcher appears:

  • On desktop: click your current role name in the top-right of the header (next to the bell icon) to open "Switch role," then pick the role you want — you'll be taken to that role's home page.
  • On mobile: the bottom navigation bar includes a role button showing your current role; tap it to open the same switcher.

If your account has only one role, no switcher is shown — you simply use that role's screens.

Profile Menu & Signing Out

Click the circular initials avatar in the top-right corner to open the profile menu. It shows your email and current role. Administrators also see a "Manage users" link here. Everyone sees "Sign out."

3.2 The Dashboard at a Glance

The Dashboard (/) is the Admin home page — a live, real-time overview of the entire operation. It is covered in full in Section 4.1, but as a navigation landmark, it contains:

  • KPI cards — Active Deliveries, In Pipeline, In Lotting, Ready for Pickup (each links to the relevant page).
  • Stage Efficiency — one number showing overall health.
  • Auction Dates panel — set/edit the date for each of the six auction colors.
  • Warehouse Floor map — a clickable grid of every zone, color-coded by what's happening in it.
  • Pipeline Distribution and Stage Velocity — visual breakdowns of where racks are and how fast they're moving.
  • Operational Alerts — the single most important panel for Supervisors/Managers; it lists everything that currently needs action.

3.4 Search & Filtering

FlowOps offers two complementary ways to find things:

Universal Search

The Search page (/search) is available to every role. Press / from anywhere in the app to jump straight to it. Type any of the following and FlowOps will find matching results across Zones, Deliveries, and Racks:

  • A rack code (e.g. RC-0042)
  • A consigner name (e.g. Smith Auctions)
  • A delivery code or J-Number (e.g. DEL-0008 or J-10294)
  • A zone name (e.g. B, W4, Hall)

Use the filter pills (All / Zones / Racks / Deliveries) once you've typed something to narrow the result list.

List-Page Filters

The Racks, Deliveries, Lotting, and other list pages each have their own filter pills and search boxes scoped to that page — e.g. the Racks page lets you filter by pipeline stage, "Needs Attention," "Held," and auction color, in addition to a free-text search box. These are covered individually in Part 4.

Part 4Feature Documentation

This part documents every page in FlowOps in detail: what it's for, what each button does, step-by-step instructions, real-world examples, and troubleshooting tips.

4.1 Dashboard (Home)

URL: /   Who uses it: Admin (Supervisors & Managers). Other roles are automatically sent to their own home page.

Overview

The Dashboard is the live "mission control" view of the entire warehouse. It updates in real time as racks move through the pipeline, deliveries arrive, and alerts fire. The header shows "{N} racks completed today" or "Live warehouse overview," plus status pills for "{N} on hold" and "{N} need attention," and a connection indicator (Live / Connecting / Offline).

Main Functions

  • KPI cards (2×2 grid): Active Deliveries, In Pipeline, In Lotting, Ready for Pickup — each is clickable and jumps to the relevant list page.
  • Stage Efficiency card: a percentage with a colored bar and a status chip — "On target" (90%+, green), "Moderate pressure" (80–89%, amber), "High pressure" (under 80%, red). This is the percentage of active racks that are not flagged Needs Attention.
  • Auction Dates panel: lists all six auction colors with their assigned date (or "—"). "Set"/"Edit" opens a date picker with Save/Cancel; "✕" clears a date.
  • Warehouse Floor map: a clickable grid of every Gallery (G1–G7, PU) and Warehouse (W1–W10, B, C, H) zone, color-coded by what's in it (see legend below). Clicking a cell opens that Zone Detail page.
  • Pipeline Distribution bar: a horizontal multi-color bar showing the proportion of active racks in each stage, with a legend and "overloaded"/"busy" tags.
  • Stage Velocity panel: one row per active stage with a progress bar (green=ok, amber=watch, orange=over) and the average time racks spend there vs. the limit.
  • Operational Alerts panel: the action list — see below.

Step-by-Step: Morning Dashboard Review

  1. Open the Dashboard. Check the connection indicator says "Live."
  2. Read the Operational Alerts panel top to bottom — this is your priority list for the day.
  3. For any "needs attention" alert, click "View →" to open Needs Attention and assign staff to clear the backlog.
  4. Check Stage Efficiency — if it's "Moderate" or "High pressure," look at the Pipeline Distribution bar to see which stage is overloaded.
  5. Scan the Warehouse Floor map for any zones that look wrong (e.g. a delivery still showing "Arrived" hours after it should be Processing).
  6. Confirm Auction Dates are set for any colors with active racks — unset dates mean no countdown warnings will fire.
ExampleThe Dashboard shows "Pickup overloaded (16/16 racks)" in red and "Lotting queue high (12 racks)" in amber. A Supervisor clicks through to Racks filtered to Pickup, confirms the PU zone is genuinely full, and asks Front Desk to hold any new scheduled pickups until racks clear. Separately, they pull an extra Sorter into the Lotting room for the afternoon to bring the queue back under 10.

Troubleshooting

  • Connection shows "Offline" or "Connecting": check your internet connection; the Dashboard will reconnect automatically and refresh data once back online.
  • A KPI number looks wrong / doesn't match what you see on the floor: remember KPIs count active (non-completed, where noted) racks/deliveries only — a rack or delivery marked Completed drops out of those counts.
  • Auction date alert isn't firing: confirm the auction color is set on the Dashboard's Auction Dates panel AND the relevant delivery/rack has that auction color assigned.

4.2 Front Desk Home

URL: /front-desk   Who uses it: Coordinators (Front Desk role)

Overview

The Coordinator's home base for the day — greets staff with "Good morning/afternoon/evening" and today's date, then provides quick actions for registering deliveries and looking up consigners.

Main Functions

  • Two big action buttons: "Walk-in" ("Truck just arrived") and "Schedule delivery" ("Book future appointment").
  • Consigner lookup: "Look up a consigner…" search box — typing 2+ characters shows up to 6 matching deliveries with status badges. A "View all results for '...'" link goes to the Consigners page.
  • Quick stats cards: Arrived, Processing, Scheduled today (only shown if non-zero).
  • Today section: all of today's active deliveries, sorted Processing → Arrived → Scheduled, each clickable to open its detail page.
  • Upcoming section: up to 5 future scheduled deliveries with their dates.

Step-by-Step: Registering a Walk-In Truck

  1. Click "Walk-in."
  2. Type the consigner's name in "Consigner name" (required).
  3. Enter their "J-Number" if known (optional, e.g. J-10294).
  4. Click "Create walk-in →". FlowOps creates the delivery (status "Arrived") and opens its detail page immediately.

Step-by-Step: Scheduling a Future Delivery

  1. Click "Schedule delivery."
  2. Enter the consigner's name and J-Number (optional).
  3. Pick the date (defaults to today — change it to the expected drop-off date).
  4. Click "Schedule delivery." You stay on the Front Desk Home; the new delivery appears in "Upcoming."
ExampleA regular consigner, "Whitmore Estates," calls to say they'll drop off a truckload next Tuesday. The Coordinator clicks "Schedule delivery," types "Whitmore Estates," enters their J-Number, picks next Tuesday's date, and clicks "Schedule delivery." When the truck actually arrives Tuesday, the Coordinator finds it under "Today," opens it, and clicks "Mark arrived."
Common MistakeCreating a brand-new consigner record for someone who has consigned before, because their name was typed slightly differently. Always check the suggestion card that appears under "Consigner name" — if a close match shows "X past deliveries · last [date]," click it to reuse the existing consigner instead of creating a duplicate.

Troubleshooting

  • Can't find a consigner in lookup: try searching by J-Number instead of name, or use the full Consigners directory (Section 4.5) which supports broader sorting/search.
  • Created a delivery with the wrong consigner name: open the delivery, click "Edit," fix the name, and "Save."

4.3 Deliveries (List)

URL: /deliveries   Who uses it: All roles (full edit rights for Admin & Front Desk)

Overview

The master log of every delivery — walk-in and scheduled, past and present. This is where new deliveries are created from anywhere in the app (not just Front Desk Home), and where you track each delivery's overall status.

Main Functions

    • Walk-in tab: Consigner name (autofocus, with duplicate-consigner suggestions), J-Number (optional). Submit: "Create walk-in →" — jumps straight to the new delivery's detail page.
    • Scheduled tab: same fields plus a date picker (defaults to today). Submit: "Create delivery" — stays on the list.
  • Filter pills: All / Arrived / Processing / Done (saved in the URL so you can bookmark/share a filtered view).
  • Delivery cards — sorted with your last-visited delivery pinned to the top, then grouped Processing → Arrived → Scheduled → Complete, then by rack count. Each card shows the J-Number/code, status badge, "walk-in" tag, "recent" tag, "N notes" tag, consigner name, date/zone info, and a colored left border by status.
  • Next-status action button on each card (e.g. "Mark arrived," "Start processing," "Mark complete") — advances the delivery one stage without opening it.

4.4 Delivery Detail

URL: /deliveries/[id]   Who uses it: All roles (delete is Admin/Supervisor only)

Overview

The complete working record for one delivery: its status, every rack it contains, notes, photos, zones, and donation/trash outcome percentages.

Main Functions

  • Header: J-Number/code, status badge, "walk-in" tag, consigner name, arrival date. "Edit" opens a form for consigner name and J-Number (Save/Cancel/Clear). Delete (trash icon, Admin/Supervisor only) requires confirming "Yes, delete."
  • Next-status button — same as the list page (Mark arrived / Start processing / Mark complete), with the same "racks still in Unpacking & Sorting" guard.
  • "← Move back to [previous status]" — lets you undo an accidental status change, with confirmation.

4.7 Create Rack (Unpack)

URL: /unpack   Who uses it: Unpacker, Sorter, Lotter, Admin

Overview

The page used to register a brand-new rack as items come off the truck — link it to the right consigner, set its starting stage and priority, give it a code, and queue its label for printing.

Main Functions — Step 1: Select Consigner

  • Search box: "Search consigner or delivery…" — filters today's/walk-in deliveries first, then alphabetically.
  • "No consigner" — proceed without linking to anyone (rare; used for operational/internal racks).
  • "+ Add consigner" — inline form: "Consigner name" (required), "J number (optional)," "Confirm" — creates a standalone consigner for this rack on the spot.

  • Rack ID (optional) — text field, auto-uppercases. Placeholder example: Yellow/Red. Pressing space auto-inserts "/" (up to 2 slashes total), then literal spaces — this builds location-style codes. Leave blank to auto-generate a code (e.g. RC-0042).

Step-by-Step: Creating a Rack for an Arriving Delivery

  1. Go to Create Rack.
  2. In Step 1, search for the consigner's name or delivery code and select it (or "+ Add consigner" if they're new and not yet in a delivery).
  3. In Step 2, pick the auction color if this auction run is already known.
  4. Leave Status at "Unpacking & Sorting" (the normal starting point).
  5. Type a Rack ID if your warehouse uses location-based codes (e.g. typing "Yellow Red" becomes YELLOW/RED), or leave blank for an auto code.
  6. Click "Create rack & add to print queue."
  7. Repeat for each rack from this delivery — they'll all appear in "Created this session."
  8. When done, click "Print N label(s)" to print all queued labels at once.
Common MistakeTyping a Rack ID that's already in use without checking the duplicate warning. If the amber "duplicate rack" card appears, don't ignore it — either pick a different ID, or if the existing rack was created in error, use its "Delete" button first.
Best PracticeAlways select the correct delivery/consigner in Step 1 before creating racks. Racks created with "No consigner" are much harder to trace later — use "+ Add consigner" instead if the consigner exists but isn't linked to a delivery yet.

Troubleshooting

  • Can't find the delivery in Step 1 search: confirm Front Desk has registered the delivery (Section 4.2/4.3) — racks can't be linked to a delivery that doesn't exist yet. Use "+ Add consigner" as a fallback if you need to start unpacking immediately.

Step-by-Step: Making "Smart" Backups a Habit

  1. Pick a cadence that matches how much you'd hate to redo it — daily for a busy warehouse, weekly or biweekly for lighter operations. Many sites tie it to an existing routine, e.g. right after the weekly reporting exports (4.17).
  2. Click "Back up now" and save the downloaded file somewhere durable — a shared drive or cloud storage folder, not just one laptop's downloads folder.
  3. Name/organize files by date (the filename already includes a timestamp) and keep a few recent backups, not just the latest one — that way a bad restore or a mistake noticed a few days later can still be recovered from an earlier point.

  1. On Reports → Backup & Restore, click "Choose backup file…" and pick your most recent good backup.

  3. Click "Confirm restore." Wait for the success message.
  4. Click "Reload now" so every page reflects the restored data.
ImportantRestoring overwrites any current record that shares an ID with the backup — if changes were made after the backup was taken, those changes are lost for that record. Restore is for recovering from data loss, not for everyday undo.
Best PracticeA backup taken before a risky bulk change (e.g. a big import or cleanup) gives you a clean rollback point if something looks wrong afterward.

5.2 Assigning Deliveries

This is the entry point for everything else — no racks can exist without a delivery (or a standalone consigner record).

  1. Truck arrives unannounced (walk-in): Front Desk uses the "Walk-in" button on Front Desk Home (4.2) or "New delivery" → Walk-in tab on the Deliveries list (4.3). Status starts at "Arrived."
  2. Truck is expected later: Front Desk uses "Schedule delivery" with a future date. Status starts at "Scheduled," and it shows up under "Upcoming" and on the Dashboard's "This week" forecast.
  3. When a scheduled truck actually arrives: open the delivery (it'll be in "Today" on Front Desk Home) and click "Mark arrived."
  4. When unpacking begins: click "Start processing" on the delivery — this is the signal for warehouse staff that racks can now be created against it.
Example"Hargrove Antiques" calls Wednesday to book a drop-off for Friday. Front Desk schedules it for Friday. Friday morning the truck arrives early — Front Desk finds it under "Today" (it shows as "Scheduled" with today's date), opens it, and clicks "Mark arrived," then "Start processing" once unloading begins.

5.3 Assigning Racks

  1. Once a delivery is "Processing," Unpacking/Sorting staff go to Create Rack (4.7).
  2. In Step 1, search for and select the delivery (or its consigner).
  3. In Step 2, set the auction color (if known), leave Status at "Unpacking & Sorting," optionally enter a location-style Rack ID, and click "Create rack & add to print queue."
  4. Repeat for every rack from this delivery — alternatively, racks can be pre-created from the delivery's own "+ Add rack" panel (4.4).
  5. Assign each rack to a physical zone — either via the Rack Detail zone quick-pick (C/B/H buttons or dropdown, 4.9) or from the Zone Detail "+ Rack" link (4.13).
  6. Print labels in a batch from the Label Print Queue (4.20) and physically attach them to each rack.
Best PracticeAssign a zone to a rack as soon as it's physically placed, not at the end of the day from memory — anyone searching for that rack (including Front Desk answering a customer call) relies on the zone being accurate in real time.

5.4 Managing Zones

  1. Before a delivery arrives: a Supervisor or Front Desk can pre-assign an empty zone as that delivery's staging area — open the zone (4.13), choose "Active delivery" or "Scheduled," and assign/schedule it.
  2. During processing: racks get assigned to that zone (or to the fixed C/B/H rooms as they move through Sorting/Lotting/Hallway) as described in 5.3.
  3. For auction staging: once a batch of racks is ready for a specific auction run, a zone can be set to that Auction color so everyone can see at a glance which run it's staging for.
  4. Freeing a zone: once a delivery is complete and its racks have moved to Pickup/Completed, use "Clear purpose" on the zone so it's available for the next delivery.
  5. The Pick-Up zone (PU) is managed separately via its drag-and-drop layout (4.13) — Pickup staff arrange racks into physical slots as they're staged for the auction floor, and click "Complete" as each rack is collected.
Common MistakeLeaving a zone's purpose set to a completed delivery. The zone then looks "occupied" on the floor map and in the "+ Add zone" dropdown for other deliveries, even though it's physically empty. Always "Clear purpose" once a delivery's racks have all moved on.

5.5 Monitoring Progress

This is primarily a Supervisor/Manager workflow, built around the Dashboard.

  1. Start at the Dashboard's Operational Alerts panel (4.1) — it's ordered roughly by severity (critical/red first).
  2. For "{N} racks need attention," click through to Needs Attention (4.11) and work the groups (4.11 step-by-step).
  3. For "Lotting queue high/overloaded," check the Lotting Queue (4.14) and consider reassigning staff.
  4. For "Pickup near capacity/overloaded," check the PU zone (4.13) — confirm racks are actually being collected on schedule, and consider pausing new racks moving to Pickup until space frees up.
  5. For auction-date warnings ("{J#}: N racks need Ready before auction"), open that delivery and confirm with the Lotting team that those racks are prioritized.
  6. Check Stage Velocity and Pipeline Distribution for early warning signs even when no alert has fired yet — a stage trending toward "watch"/"busy" today may be tomorrow's alert.

5.6 Completing Work

  1. Per rack: as work finishes at each stage, staff click "Move to {Next Stage} →" (from the Racks queue, Rack Detail, or Lotting Queue's "Start lotting"/"Mark ready" buttons) until the rack reaches Completed (typically via "Complete" in the Pick-Up layout, 4.13).
  2. Per delivery: once every rack from a delivery has moved past Unpacking & Sorting, click "Mark complete" on the delivery (4.3/4.4). If any racks are still in Unpacking & Sorting, FlowOps blocks this with a warning — finish those first.
  3. Record the outcome: on the completed delivery, use "Set outcome" to enter Donation % (Trash % comes from the Dumpsters page) — this gives Managers a Sellable % for reporting.

5.7 Reviewing Metrics

  1. Admins/Managers open Reports (4.17).
  2. Export "All Racks" and "Stage Durations" to review how long items spent at each stage this cycle — useful for staffing decisions.
  3. Export "Dumpsters" for trash billing reconciliation.
  4. Export "All Deliveries" for a cycle summary.
  5. Cross-reference with the Consigners directory (4.5) — review "Frequently held" and "Slow to process" tags for any consigner-specific process issues.
  6. Once exports are done, take a data backup (4.22).

Part 6Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What's the difference between a "delivery" and a "rack"?

A delivery is the truck/drop-off event (one consigner, one visit). A rack is a physical unit of items from that delivery. One delivery usually produces several racks.

Q: What does the J-Number mean, and do I always need one?

The J-Number is Quinn's internal customer/job number (e.g. J-10294). It's optional when creating a delivery or consigner — FlowOps will generate its own delivery/rack codes either way — but entering it whenever known makes consigner lookups and billing much easier.

Q: I have more than one role — how do I switch?

Click your role name in the header (desktop, top-right) or the role button in the bottom nav (mobile) and choose "Switch role." See Section 3.1.

Q: What does the blue "HOLD" badge mean, and who can clear it?

It means a rack is intentionally paused, with a recorded reason. Any role except Front Desk can apply or clear a hold from the Rack Detail page (4.9). Held racks don't count as "needs attention."

Q: A rack shows a red, pulsing "needs attention" badge — what should I do?

It's overdue for its current stage and high priority — the most urgent combination. Open it, do the work if possible, or apply a Hold with the real reason if it's genuinely blocked (4.9, 4.11).

Q: What's a "mixed" rack?

A rack containing items from more than one consigner. Use "Consigner +" on Rack Detail (4.9) to add additional consigners — each gets its own printable label.

Q: How do auction colors work — do I have to set one?

No, it's optional, but if your warehouse uses weekly color-coded auction runs, tag racks/zones with the relevant color (Red/Orange/Yellow/Green/Blue/Black) so the Dashboard's Auction Dates panel and countdown warnings work correctly (4.1).

Q: I created a rack with the wrong consigner — can I fix it?

Yes. Open the rack, click "Edit," and search for the correct delivery/consigner, or pick "— Custom consigner (no delivery) —" (4.9).

Q: What happens if I click "Move to {Next Stage}" by mistake?

Use the "←" revert button on Rack Detail to move it back one stage (requires confirmation). This isn't available to Front Desk. For deliveries, use "← Move back to [previous status]" (4.4).

Q: Why can't I mark a delivery "Complete"?

FlowOps blocks this if any of its racks are still in Unpacking & Sorting. Finish sorting those racks (or fix their delivery assignment if they were logged incorrectly) first (4.3/4.4).

Q: How do I print labels for several racks at once?

Leave "Add to print queue" checked when creating racks (4.7), or use the "Queue"/"Add to queue" button on any rack. Then go to the Label Print Queue (4.20) and click "Print all."

Q: The Scan page won't open my camera — what's wrong?

Your browser/device needs camera permission for FlowOps. Allow it in your device settings, then reopen Scan (4.19).

Q: What's the difference between "Ready" and "Pickup" stages?

Both are "waiting stages" — it's normal for racks to sit there. "Ready" means lotted and waiting for its auction date; "Pickup" means physically staged in the Pick-Up zone for the auction floor. Neither triggers "needs attention" on the same short timelines as the active stages.

Q: Why does the Pickup zone have a 16-rack limit?

16 racks is the Pick-Up zone's capacity for a full auction cycle (matching the Pick-Up Layout's slot count). The Dashboard warns at 13 ("near capacity") and 16 ("overloaded") so staff can react before the floor is physically full (4.1, 4.13).

Q: Where do I record trash/donation amounts?

Trash % is logged per-delivery on the Dumpsters page (4.16), which automatically appears as "Trash %" on that delivery's Outcome section. Donation % is entered directly on the delivery (4.4).

Q: Can Front Desk staff move racks between stages?

No. Front Desk has view-only access to racks — no "Move to...", revert, hold, or delete buttons appear for them. They can still create/edit deliveries, add zones, notes, and photos.

Q: How do I get a new employee set up?

An Administrator goes to User Management (4.21), clicks "Invite user," enters their email, selects the role(s) matching their job, and sends the invite. The employee receives a magic-link email to set their password.

8.3 Auction Colors & Dates

  • The six auction colors (Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Black) are fixed in the system — they represent Quinn's recurring weekly auction-run naming convention.
  • Each week, set or update the date for the colors currently in use via the Dashboard's Auction Dates panel (4.1). This date drives the Dashboard's auction-deadline warnings ("{J#}: N racks need Ready before auction").
  • Clear a color's date ("✕") once that auction has passed and before reusing the color for a future cycle, to avoid stale countdowns.

8.4 Weekly Auction Cycle — Close-Out Checklist

  1. Confirm all racks that should be Completed for this cycle actually show Completed status (check Racks, filtered, and/or the PU layout for any racks not yet marked "Complete").
  2. On Reports (4.17), export All Racks and Stage Durations for this cycle's performance data.
  3. Export Dumpsters for trash billing.
  4. Export All Deliveries if a customer-facing or management summary is needed.
  5. Review Consigners "Frequently held" / "Slow to process" flags for any process follow-ups.
  6. Update Auction Dates (8.3) for the next cycle's colors.
  7. Take a data backup (4.22) once this cycle's exports are done.
  8. Audit and clear stale Zone purposes (8.2) so the floor map is accurate for the new cycle.

8.6 Backup & Restore Schedule

FlowOps does not back itself up automatically — Backup & Restore (4.22) on the Reports page is a manual export/import that an Administrator runs.

CadenceWhen it fits
DailyBusy warehouses with high volume — minimizes how much work could be lost if something goes wrong overnight or mid-week.
WeeklyPairs naturally with the cycle close-out checklist (8.4) — back up once that week's exports and reviews are done.
BiweeklyLighter operations where data changes slowly; still gives a recovery point within a couple weeks of any incident.
  • Store downloaded backup files outside of any single device — a shared drive or cloud storage folder that multiple Administrators can access.
  • Keep more than one recent backup. If a problem isn't noticed immediately, the most recent backup may already include the bad data — an older one may be needed instead.
  • Delivery photos and user/role accounts are not included in these backups (4.22) — they're managed separately (Supabase Storage and User Management, 4.21).
